01 — A place of contrasts

Small country.
Big stories.

Albania sits where the Adriatic and Ionian seas meet the Balkans. In a single journey, the landscape can shift from a sunlit coastline to dramatic peaks and green valleys.

Its best-known destinations are only part of the story. Away from the busiest paths, old stone towns, clear-water springs and welcoming villages offer a slower way to discover the country—and a glimpse of the traditions that make it special.

02 — Worth the detour

Three places to
look a little closer.

Each stop reveals a different side of Albania: its history, its landscapes and the people who call them home.

01
UNESCO town · Southern Albania

Gjirokastër

Known as the “Stone City,” Gjirokastër stands in the Drinos River valley in southern Albania. Its historic centre is part of a UNESCO World Heritage site.

  • Look for: the 13th-century citadel, old bazaar and traditional tower houses.
  • Why it is special: many of its distinctive two-storey houses date from the 17th century.
  • Good to know: steep cobbled streets connect the old town and castle.

02
Natural wonder · Near Sarandë

The Blue Eye

The Blue Eye is a natural spring near the village of Muzinë, about 18 kilometres from Sarandë. Its deep-blue centre and green edges create the appearance of a giant eye.

  • Look for: the vivid blue pool, forest paths and small streams.
  • Why it is special: the spring is a protected natural monument.
  • Good to know: the water stays very cold, even in summer.

03
Mountain village · Albanian Alps

Theth

Theth is a mountain village in northern Albania, set among the Albanian Alps. It is known for its dramatic valley, traditional houses and welcoming local culture.

  • Look for: Grunas Waterfall, Grunas Canyon and the Okoli Cold Springs.
  • Why it is special: the village preserves centuries-old alpine traditions.
  • Good to know: mountain roads and trails can be challenging; plan for the season.
